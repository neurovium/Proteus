# The Alignment Computation: Pipeline Design (v2)

*Companion to `proteus_meta_model.yaml`. This document specifies the computation that proposes semantic mappings between registered schemas. Every stage is stated in terms of what it reads from the registry and what it writes back, so the pipeline and the meta-model form a closed loop: the registry feeds the matcher, the matcher's output populates the mapping layer, and the curation lifecycle feeds labels back into the matcher.*

*Changes from v1: a seventh signal — **declared semantics via OWL reasoning** over ontology anchors — added as a candidate-generation channel and a scoring feature, with profile enrichment in Stage 0 and reasoner-materialized entailment (ELK, escalating to HermiT-class if needed); an explicit **reasoner-based logical repair loop** in Stage 5 over the merged candidate mapping set; the structural signal reformulated as **personalized-PageRank diffusion on the product graph**, replacing the ad hoc round count; **landmark diffusion coordinates** over shared anchors as an optional cross-schema structural feature; and **diffusion over the mapping graph** as the principled basis for transitive confidence in hub composition.*

## Overview

The pipeline runs in six stages plus a composition step. Stage 0 assembles, for every registry entity, the evidence bundle that all matching operates on. Stage 1 generates candidate pairs cheaply, at high recall. Stage 2 computes a vector of independent similarity signals per candidate pair. Stage 3 combines those signals into a single calibrated confidence. Stage 4 assigns the graded mapping predicate. Stage 5 repairs global incoherences in the candidate set. Stage 6 writes the result into the registry as a `MappingSet` of `PROPOSED` mappings. Composition then derives schema-to-schema mappings from schema-to-hub mappings, so that direct pairwise matching is the exception rather than the rule.

A design principle worth stating once, because the new declared-semantics signal makes it concrete: the six statistical signals *infer* what a schema author meant; an ontology anchor is the author *telling us*. The two kinds of evidence have opposite profiles — statistical signals are dense but noisy; declared semantics are sparse but near-deterministic, with a distinctive failure mode (annotation error rather than graded noise). The pipeline keeps them separate all the way to the provenance record, so a mapping that rests on declared semantics is never confused with one that rests on statistics.

## Stage 0 — Matching profiles

Each entity gets a *matching profile* assembled from the registry: `name`, `source_native_id`, `aliases`, `definition`, the parent class name, sibling property names, `value_type`, the unit canonicalized to its QUDT dimension vector, and, for enumerated properties, the ValueSet's permissible values.

**New: anchor enrichment.** If the entity carries ontology annotations (`exact_mappings`, `close_mappings`, `broad_mappings` with CURIEs such as `ivoa:pos.eq.ra`), the profile is enriched with the referenced term's own label, synonyms, and definition, fetched from the resolved ontology version and cached. The enrichment is recorded in a separate `anchor_text` field of the profile rather than merged into the author's text, so downstream signals can weight authored and inherited text differently — but both feed the embedding and the lexical token set. The payoff is asymmetric and compounding: a cosmologist who wrote `ra_deg` with no description inherits "angular distance measured eastward along the celestial equator" as searchable text, and an *unannotated* `right_asc` in another schema can now reach it through that inherited definition. One group's annotation effort improves matching for everyone — the adoption gradient the registry wants, without mandating ontology literacy.

Two normalization details matter more than they look. First, identifiers are tokenized by splitting snake_case and camelCase before any comparison — `z_spec` must become `{z, spec}` or lexical matching is blind to it. Second, the pipeline maintains an abbreviation dictionary (`spec → spectroscopic`, `RA → right ascension`) applied during normalization. This dictionary grows as a side-product of curation: every accepted mapping whose `match_string` exhibits an abbreviation pattern is a candidate dictionary entry. Over time it quietly becomes one of the project's most valuable field-specific assets.

**New: the anchor index.** Alongside the profiles, Stage 0 materializes, once per referenced ontology version, its *classified hierarchy*: the transitive closure of `subClassOf` / `skos:broader` over the ontology's terms, computed by an EL reasoner (ELK) or, for SKOS-ish vocabularies, plain transitive closure. This turns every later entailment question ("is anchor X subsumed by anchor Y?") into a hash lookup. A full SROIQ tableau reasoner (HermiT) is not needed here — LinkML-compiled OWL is shallow and IVOA vocabularies are thesaurus-like; the interface is specified as a "classified hierarchy provider" so a heavier reasoner can be swapped in if a hub ontology ever grows axioms rich enough to need one.

## Stage 1 — Candidate generation (blocking)

The pipeline never scores all pairs. Scoring |A|×|B| pairs burns compute on the overwhelmingly non-matching majority and, worse, generates plausible-looking noise that pollutes the review queue. Three retrieval channels run and their results are unioned:

1. **Embedding channel.** Each profile (including anchor enrichment) is embedded — sentence-transformers locally, or an embedding API — into an approximate-nearest-neighbor index (FAISS or hnswlib), and the top-k ≈ 20 neighbors are taken per entity.
2. **Lexical channel.** A TF-IDF inverted index over the tokens of names, aliases, and native IDs, which catches rare exact tokens that embeddings occasionally smear.
3. **New: anchor channel.** All pairs whose anchor sets intersect (shared CURIE) or are entailment-related in the anchor index (one anchor subsumes the other in the referenced ontology) are injected as candidates directly. This channel can surface pairs that neither statistical channel would find — two elements with disjoint vocabularies and no descriptions but the same declared IVOA concept — and its candidates carry a flag so Stage 2 knows their origin.

The union then passes through the **unit veto as a blocking filter**: candidate pairs whose QUDT dimension vectors are incommensurable are dropped before scoring. Vetoed pairs with high embedding scores are logged rather than discarded silently — an occasional audit of these is worthwhile, because a strong semantic match killed by the veto sometimes indicates a unit annotation *error* in the source schema rather than a false match, which is itself valuable registry feedback. The veto applies to anchor-channel candidates too: a shared anchor on dimensionally incommensurable quantities is a loud annotation bug, logged at high priority.

This stage is tuned for recall — 0.95 or better against the growing gold set — because nothing downstream can recover a pair that blocking discarded.

## Stage 2 — Signal computation

For each surviving pair the pipeline computes a feature vector, not a single score. The signals are kept separate precisely because their disagreements are informative.

*Lexical.* Best Jaro-Winkler and token-Jaccard over the cross-product of the two label sets (names × aliases × native IDs, post-normalization). Cheap, high precision on the easy cases.

*Semantic.* Cosine similarity on full-profile embeddings, and separately on definition-only embeddings. Keeping the two apart matters: a high definition-cosine paired with low label similarity is exactly the `z_spec` ↔ `redshift_spectroscopic` signature the system is built to catch. With anchor enrichment in play, a third variant — anchor-text-only cosine — is computed when both sides have anchors, so the model can learn how much of a semantic match is authored versus inherited.

*Type and unit.* Categorical features: datatype identical, castable, or incompatible; unit identical or convertible (incommensurable pairs were vetoed in Stage 1). "Convertible but not identical" is not a penalty — it is evidence for exact-match-after-conversion, and the conversion factor computed here is precisely what a `Transform` record should capture: a Transform is the natural artifact of an accepted convertible mapping.

*Value-level.* When ValueSets or instance data exist: Jaccard overlap over permissible values for enums (or over their `value_meaning` IRIs where annotated); for numeric properties with data samples, a Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic after unit conversion. Ranges that fail to overlap after conversion are soft negative evidence.

*Structural — reformulated as diffusion.* v1 ran similarity flooding "for two to three rounds" to let property-level matches boost class-level matches while avoiding fixpoint pathologies. That truncation was an ad hoc damping; v2 replaces it with the principled version of the same computation. Build the pairwise product graph (nodes are candidate pairs, edges connect pairs whose members are neighbors in their respective schemas), seed it with the lexical + semantic pair scores, and run **personalized PageRank**: diffusion with restart probability α back to the seed distribution. This converges to a well-defined damped fixpoint, and α becomes an interpretable knob — how far structural evidence is allowed to propagate — replacing the arbitrary round count. (Similarity flooding *is* power iteration on this graph; PPR is the same computation with guaranteed convergence and one tunable parameter. Heat-kernel diffusion with time parameter t is an equivalent alternative.) The class-level structural score remains the value of the optimal bipartite assignment over the two classes' property-pair diffused scores (Hungarian algorithm). Schema graphs are small, so the linear system solves directly with sparse methods — no iteration-count tuning at all.

*New: declared semantics — the reasoner signal.* This is the seventh method, and it is the one place in per-pair scoring where an OWL reasoner participates. The features are categorical, read from the reasoner-materialized anchor index of Stage 0: anchors identical; anchors entailment-related (with direction — subject's anchor broader or narrower than object's, as *entailed* by the referenced ontology, not merely asserted: the reasoner closes over asserted subclass axioms, equivalences, and property restrictions, so `spec_z ⊑ redshift` is found even when the ontology states it indirectly); anchors present on both sides but provably unrelated or disjoint (soft negative evidence: both authors declared intent, and the intents differ); anchors absent on one or both sides (feature missing, not zero — see Stage 3). This signal is an assertion of intent, not an inference, and it is treated accordingly: very high weight when present, but never a hard override, because an annotation is only as good as its annotator. A pair with identical anchors that a reviewer nonetheless REJECTs is disproportionately valuable — it is the system's annotation-bug detector, and such rejections are flagged back to the source schema's maintainers.

*Optional: landmark diffusion coordinates.* Spectral quantities (Laplacian eigenvectors, diffusion distances) are not comparable across two independent graphs — different spectra, sign and ordering ambiguities — which is why raw spectral features are not used. But the anchor set and existing hub mappings provide *shared landmarks*, and cross-graph comparability returns: compute each element's diffusion distance to every shared landmark, giving each element coordinates in a common space ("close to the coordinate-frame anchor, far from the instrument anchor" means the same thing in both schemas), and use the distance between coordinate vectors as a feature. This inherits the sparsity of the anchor signal — it is only as dense as the annotations plus accepted hub mappings — and arrives via the same adoption gradient. It is specified as optional: implement after the seven core signals, evaluate against the held-out set, and keep only if it adds precision the structural diffusion does not already capture. On small tree-like schema graphs it may not; on larger, denser schemas it should.

## Stage 3 — Combination and calibration

At cold start, the signals are combined by a hand-weighted logistic function, deliberately conservative, and a seed gold set is built from the trivial cases — identical native IDs, identical labels with identical units, and now *identical anchors with compatible units* — which a human can confirm in an hour. Anchor-identical pairs are the fastest-growing part of this seed set and the cheapest to review.

From then on, the curation lifecycle *is* the training loop. `ACCEPTED` mappings become positive labels; `REJECTED` mappings become negative labels; a logistic regression or gradient-boosted model is retrained on the feature vectors, and its output is **calibrated** with isotonic regression so that `confidence` genuinely means P(correct). Calibration is non-negotiable: the registry's query semantics ("include PROPOSED above 0.8") silently assume calibrated probabilities, and an uncalibrated 0.8 is a lie the entire downstream knowledge graph inherits. This is also where retained rejections compound — they supply most of the hard negatives, and they act as a negative cache: Stage 6 never re-proposes a pair that carries a `REJECTED` mapping.

**Missingness is a first-class concern now.** The declared-semantics and landmark features are absent for most pairs (most schemas will carry no annotations). Absent is not zero: a gradient-boosted model handles missing values natively; for the logistic model, explicit presence indicators are added. Calibration must also be checked *conditionally* — confidence should mean P(correct) both for anchor-supported pairs and for purely statistical ones, since the two populations have very different base rates. If a single calibrator distorts either population, fit separate isotonic curves per evidence regime.

An optional but high-leverage addition is LLM adjudication as a second-stage scorer on pairs above threshold. The prompt contains both full profiles (including anchor text); the model returns a structured verdict — one of {exact, close, broad, narrow, related, none} — plus a one-sentence rationale. The verdict serves both as an additional feature here and as the predicate proposal for Stage 4; the rationale is stored in `mapping_comment` for the reviewer. Current models are genuinely strong at exactly the definition-subsumption judgment ("is spectroscopic redshift a *kind of* redshift, or the *same as* redshift?") that classical matchers handle worst.

## Stage 4 — Predicate assignment

Match/no-match is insufficient; the graded vocabulary must be decided. The policy is asymmetric by design. Machines default to `CLOSE_MATCH`. `EXACT_MATCH` requires convergent evidence: identical-or-convertible units *and* high definition similarity *and* an LLM verdict of "exact" — or, new in v2, *identical anchors* with compatible units and no contradicting signal, which is the one machine pathway to `EXACT_MATCH` that does not require the LLM, because it rests on declared intent rather than inference. Broad and narrow matches come from asymmetric cues — definition subsumption per the LLM, one entity's slot-assignment set strictly containing the mapped counterparts of the other's, or *entailed subsumption between anchors* in the anchor index, which is the cleanest broad/narrow evidence the system has and sets the direction unambiguously. Humans promote to exact; `EQUIVALENT_CLASS` is exclusively human-issued.

This conservatism is cheap insurance. An over-claimed exactMatch propagates through composition; an under-claimed closeMatch merely waits for a reviewer.

## Stage 5 — Coherence repair

Before write-back, the candidate set is checked for global consistency. Within a schema pair, exactMatch should be nearly one-to-one: where one entity exactMatches two targets, the best-scoring mapping is kept and the rest are demoted to closeMatch. Through the hub, one entity exactMatching two *sibling* hub concepts is a contradiction — this is not auto-resolved but flagged as a conflict pair at the top of the review queue. Such conflicts are disproportionately informative: they usually reveal either a hub concept that needs splitting or a source schema conflating two distinct quantities.

The anchor index adds one new check: a proposed exactMatch between entities whose anchors are entailment-*disjoint* in a shared ontology (declared as siblings or otherwise incompatible) is demoted and flagged — either the match is wrong or an annotation is, and a human decides which.

**Reasoner-based logical repair.** Beyond the structural checks, the pipeline includes an explicit reasoner pass over the merged candidate set, run as follows: export the two schemas via `gen-owl`, add the referenced ontology modules (extracted as locality-based modules around the anchors, not the full ontologies), translate the candidate mappings into OWL axioms (`EXACT_MATCH` → `owl:equivalentClass`/`equivalentProperty`, `BROAD_MATCH`/`NARROW_MATCH` → subsumption in the appropriate direction; `CLOSE_MATCH` and `RELATED_MATCH` are *not* translated — SKOS deliberately carries no OWL semantics for them, and pretending otherwise manufactures false incoherence), and classify the merged ontology. Every unsatisfiable class or property indicts a minimal subset of the candidate mappings; the reasoner's explanation facility identifies that subset, the lowest-confidence member is demoted to `CLOSE_MATCH` or flagged, and the pass repeats until coherent. This is the diagnose-and-repair loop LogMap performs natively; running it in-house against the anchor modules keeps it aligned with the registry's own provenance records.

Reasoner choice follows the profile of what is being checked: ELK suffices as long as the merged ontology stays within OWL 2 EL (which LinkML-generated OWL plus IVOA-style vocabularies typically do); the interface is the same "classified hierarchy + explanation" contract as Stage 0, so a HermiT-class SROIQ reasoner drops in without pipeline changes if a hub or referenced ontology ever accumulates disjointness axioms, cardinality restrictions, or other constructs beyond EL. That escalation path is specified now precisely so it never forces a redesign later. Repairs performed by this pass are recorded with `LOGICAL_REASONING` justification and the explanation in `mapping_comment`, so a reviewer can see *which axioms* made the set incoherent.

## Stage 6 — Write-back

One matcher run produces one `MappingSet`, with the tool name, version, and a hash of the run parameters recorded in the set description — runs become reproducible artifacts, and a miscalibrated run can be `SUPERSEDED` wholesale. Each mapping lands as `PROPOSED`, with `mapping_justification` set according to the dominant signal — `LexicalMatching`, `SemanticSimilarityThresholdMatching`, `StructuralMatching`, `LogicalReasoning` for anchor-entailment-derived mappings, or `CompositeMatching` when the ensemble decided — the evidence in `match_string`, the calibrated `confidence`, and the LLM rationale in the comment. Keeping `LogicalReasoning` distinct is what keeps provenance honest about which mappings rest on declared semantics versus statistics; per-justification precision tracking (below) depends on it.

Anchor-entailed mappings enter as high-confidence `PROPOSED`, never auto-`ACCEPTED`. The review cost is trivial (a reviewer confirms identical-anchor pairs in seconds) and the discipline preserves the invariant that everything in the high-trust layer passed a human — including the annotation-bug detection described in Stage 2.

Idempotence rules: pairs with an existing `ACCEPTED` mapping are skipped, unless the new evidence is materially stronger, in which case the new mapping is proposed alongside for reconciliation; `REJECTED` pairs are never re-proposed; and on schema re-registration, only entities with new hash_ids enter the queue at all — the meta-model's monotone-growth property doing its job.

## Composition through the hub

Once schema-to-hub mappings exist, schema-to-schema mappings are *derived*, not computed. A→hub→B composes by a strict table: exact ∘ exact = exact; exact ∘ close = close; close ∘ close = do not derive (or, at most, derive as relatedMatch at sharply decayed confidence, for review only). Derived mappings are ordinary records with `LOGICAL_REASONING` justification and `PROPOSED` status — never silently materialized as accepted, because composition launders uncertainty.

**New: diffusion over the mapping graph.** The composition table is a two-hop rule; the principled generalization is diffusion over the mapping graph itself — schemas' entities as nodes, accepted and proposed SSSOM edges weighted by calibrated confidence. Personalized PageRank from an entity, restricted to edges above a rigor threshold, yields a transitive-relatedness score for every reachable entity, with confidence decaying naturally along paths and reinforcement when multiple independent paths agree — which the hand-written table cannot express. Two uses, deliberately limited: (a) *review-queue ranking* — high diffusion score with no direct mapping is exactly the "you probably want to map these" prompt for curators; (b) *hub health metrics* — comparing diffusion-derived A↔B relatedness against directly computed spot-check matches, where systematic disagreement localizes hub concepts that are too coarse. Diffusion scores are never written back as mapping confidences; the composition table remains the only mechanism that mints derived mapping records, precisely because its conservatism is auditable and its outputs are reviewable one edge at a time.

Composition also defines the registration workflow: each new schema is matched against the hub only, and direct schema-pair matching becomes an occasional validation exercise. Spot-checking that composed A↔B mappings agree with directly computed ones — and tracking the disagreement rate — is the best ongoing health metric for the hub itself.

## Concrete stack

All Python: `rapidfuzz` for lexical similarity; `sentence-transformers` with `hnswlib` (or FAISS) for embedding retrieval; `scipy.sparse` for the PPR linear solves and `networkx` for graph assembly, with `scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment` for the Hungarian step; `scikit-learn` for signal combination and isotonic calibration; `sssom-py` for write-back I/O. For the reasoner components: `oaklib` (or `pronto`/`owlready2`) to load referenced ontologies; ELK via `robot reason` (or plain transitive closure in `networkx` for SKOS vocabularies) to materialize the Stage 0 classified hierarchies — run once per ontology version at ingestion, cached as flat parent-closure tables; `robot extract` for the locality-based anchor modules; and for the Stage 5 repair loop, `robot reason --reasoner ELK` plus `robot explain` for unsatisfiability explanations, with HermiT as the drop-in escalation for beyond-EL ontologies. The reasoner never runs per candidate pair — Stage 0 amortizes it to once per ontology version, Stage 5 to once per matcher run.

Off-the-shelf matchers slot in as additional ensemble members rather than competitors: DeepOnto/BERTMap is worth one run as a reference baseline, and LogMap and AgreementMakerLight likewise — each is fed the `gen-owl` export of the registered schemas, and each's alignment output is converted to SSSOM and lands as its own `PROPOSED` mapping set. LogMap's output is particularly interesting to compare against the declared-semantics channel, since both exploit logical structure.

Evaluation runs on a held-out slice of curated mappings, tracked as precision and recall *per justification type* — now including `LogicalReasoning` as its own line, which directly measures annotation quality across the registry. The per-method breakdown identifies which ensemble members to trust, and eventually which to drop.

## Build order

Resist building the fully automated version first. The v2 sequence:

1. Blocking + lexical/unit signals + the review loop. Get fifty curated mappings.
2. **The anchor channel and declared-semantics features** — earlier than embeddings in v2, because wherever annotations exist this is the cheapest high-precision signal in the system, it fattens the seed gold set, and its infrastructure (ontology resolution, classified hierarchies) is needed for profile enrichment anyway.
3. Embeddings (with anchor-enriched profiles from day one) and the semantic features.
4. Structural diffusion, learning, and calibration.
5. Optional: landmark coordinates and mapping-graph diffusion, each gated on measured improvement against the held-out set.

Every stage after Stage 1 is only as good as the labels the curation loop feeds it, and the loop is the part with the longest lead time — it involves humans. The anchor channel jumps the queue precisely because it shortens that lead time.
